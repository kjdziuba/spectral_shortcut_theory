\section{Conditional geometry: capacity-induced curvature disparity}
\label{sec:geometry}

\begin{theorem}[Capacity-induced module curvature disparity]
\label{thm:hessian}
Consider $F_{\thetap,\phip}=g_\phip\circ f_\thetap$ with a fixed-dimensional
linear encoder (Assumption~\ref{ass:linear}) and a family of spatial
networks $g^{(M)}_\phip$ indexed by width $M$. Suppose
Assumptions~\ref{ass:subg} and~\ref{ass:inputlip} hold, and suppose
$g_\phip$ ends in a dense classifier head over penultimate features
$h_n\in\R^{M_h}$ with $M_h=\Theta(M)$ whose softmax-weighted mean Gram
$S_h^p:=N^{-1}\sum_n p^{(n)}_{\min}h_nh_n^\top$ satisfies
$\lambda_{\max}(S_h^p)\ge q_hM_h$ with probability at least $1-\delta_h$
over the initialization, for constants $q_h>0$, $\delta_h<1$ independent
of $M$ (the \emph{head hypothesis}: the features carry a common direction of
$\Theta(M)$ energy across inputs on pixels whose initial predictions are
non-degenerate). Then there are constants $c_1,c_2>0$, depending on the
data, the initialization scheme, $K$, $S$ and the architecture class but not
on $M$ or $\Cg$, such that in expectation over Kaiming initialization
\[
  \Dcurv^{(M)}
  =\frac{\lambda_{\max}(G^{(M)}_{\phip\phip})}{\lambda_{\max}(G_{\thetap\thetap})}
  \;\ge\; c_1M-c_2 .
\]
For families with $\Cg=\Theta(M^2)$, equivalently
$\Dcurv^{(M)}\ge c_1'\sqrt{\Cg/\Cf}-c_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral block cap]
\label{lem:opcap}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:linear}--\ref{ass:inputlip},
$\lambda_{\max}(G_{\thetap\thetap})\le\|J_\thetap\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\le\widetilde L^{\,2}\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_X)=:C_\thetap$, a constant
independent of $M$, $\Cg$ and $N$.
\end{lemma}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is elementary. The rank-one
head-weight perturbation $V=c\,u^\top$, with $c$ a unit class contrast and
$u$ the dominant feature direction, gives the deterministic inequality
$\lambda_{\max}(G_{\phip\phip})\ge\lambda_{\max}(S_h^p)$, and the head
hypothesis supplies $\Omega(M)$; the cap follows from the chain rule
$J_\thetap=(\partial\hat y/\partial Z)(\partial Z/\partial\thetap)$, whose
second factor has squared operator norm exactly $\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_X)$
under linearity; the rate matches \citet{karakida2019universal} where that
law applies.

\paragraph{The hypothesis boundary.}
The theorem measures the downstream module's \emph{readiness to move}, not
the usefulness of its features: the width-linear spike exists before any
feature is learned. It concerns the top of the spatial spectrum only, is not
a dynamical claim, and is stated for the standard parameterization
\citep{yang2021tensor}. The head hypothesis is a cross-input correlation
condition on $\Theta(M)$ penultimate features: met by dense per-pixel heads
on CNN and ViT backbones, and structurally \emph{unmet} by the production
model of Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, whose classifier input has a fixed
dimension at every width, so that the theorem has no premise there rather
than a counterexample.

\paragraph{Empirical check (Experiment 1.1).}
We measure both blocks of $G$ by power iteration on a synthetic
spectral--spatial problem ($S=64$, $K=16$, two classes; five seeds per
width; Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}). For the shallow verified instance
(linear encoder, two-layer ReLU head) under default fan-in initialization,
$\lambda_{\max}(G_{\phip\phip})$ rises from $0.62$ to $46.0$ over
$M=16\to8192$ (log--log slope $0.71$) while
$\lambda_{\max}(G_{\thetap\thetap})\approx0.02$ is flat (slope $-0.02$), so
$\Dcurv$ grows from $40$ to $2405$. Under the theorem's own initialization
(He weights with a width-independent bias variance) the slope is $0.90$
($2.1\to442$) with $\lambda_{\max}(G_{\thetap\thetap})\approx0.6$ flat. A
deeper head separates the two regimes sharply: over $M=16\to1024$ its
spatial slope is $0.105$ under default initialization and $0.989$ under
the theorem's, and isolation arms (fixed bias variance alone $0.924$; He
weights with scaled or zero bias $0.696$, $0.606$) show that the floor
survives when either variance term of the head hypothesis is
width-independent, while default fan-in initialization makes both vanish
with width. Slopes are finite-range fits quoted with their levels; $\Dcurv$
is not used to attribute differences across arms, whose denominators differ.
