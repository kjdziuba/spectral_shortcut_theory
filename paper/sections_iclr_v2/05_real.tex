\section{Real centre spectra with constructed context}
\label{sec:real}

\paragraph{What this experiment is.}
We test contextual competition using measured centre spectra and their
recorded labels, with a synthetic class-associated spatial intensity
pattern along training-estimated principal directions in the
neighbourhood. It is a real-centre-spectrum experiment with constructed
context, not a validation on natural tissue context: it asks whether the
mechanism operates when the spectral cue is a real, high-dimensional class
contrast, and it cannot show that natural neighbourhoods produce it.

\paragraph{Data and readiness.}
Breast-tissue QCL micro-array cores, fold~0 of a five-fold patient-level
split; the binary task Cancer Epithelium versus Cancer-Associated Stroma
on $942$-channel pixel vectors; $24{,}000$ class-balanced training
centres, evaluation on held-out validation patients with test patients
reported separately (Appendix~\ref{app:exp3}). A readiness scan
(Table~\ref{tab:exp3_scan}) shows that with per-feature standardization a
random encoder $W\in\R^{12\times942}$ already reads this contrast at
$0.955$ against an oracle of $0.969$, because the class-mean contrast
lies along the top principal directions; PCA whitening estimated on the
training patients reduces the random-encoder probe to $0.54$ while the
oracle stays at $0.944$. We therefore run two regimes on the same pixels
and labels: \emph{ready} (standardized inputs, nothing for the encoder to
learn) and \emph{unready} (whitened inputs, the theorem's case).

\paragraph{Construction.}
Each patch is a real centre with eight real donor spectra drawn
independently of the centre label from the same split side; donor $d$
carries $\gamma_d(c\,s+\tau\eta_d)$ along the $d$th principal coordinate,
where $c$ is the centre's label, $s=\pm1$ or $c$ is replaced by an
independent label, and $\gamma_d$ is large relative to the donors'
natural variation ($3\sqrt{\lambda_d}$ standardized; $30$ in whitened
units, with a $\gamma=10$ variant added after the first results).
$\tau$ is calibrated per regime so that the neighbourhood oracle equals
the centre-only oracle ($0.969$ ready, $0.948$ unready); through a random
encoder the neighbourhood reads at $0.94$--$0.99$ in both regimes. The
model is the linear encoder over the nine spectra and a ReLU head of
width $M$ over the encoded patch; training and thresholds are those of
Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}; the probe is fitted on disjoint training
pixels and evaluated on validation centres.

\paragraph{Results.}

In the \emph{ready} regime the probe is $0.91$--$0.94$ before training and
does not move at $L^\ast$ in any arm (gain $-0.015$ to $+0.035$), yet
every jointly trained model predicts from context only: reversal accuracy
$0.08$--$0.10$ and context-random accuracy at chance for widths $8$--$512$
and for $\kappa=1$, $1/16$, $1/256$ alike, all fitting within $70$--$230$
steps; the uninformative-context comparator reaches reversal $0.93$ and
context-random $0.92$, and the frozen encoder $0.09$
(Table~\ref{tab:exp3_ready}). Nothing can be starved here; the reliance is
head-level competition between a large-amplitude cue and an $O(1)$ one,
which the theorem does not address, and it is complete.

In the \emph{unready} regime the probe starts at $0.56$--$0.60$ and at
$L^\ast$ has gained at most $0.05$ in any arm, with reversal $0.07$ and
context-random at chance; the fit takes $69$--$185$ steps even at
$\kappa=1/256$ (Table~\ref{tab:exp3_unready}). Slowing the head does not
help the encoder here because, at amplitude $30$, the contextual cue
dominates the encoder's own gradient: the encoder learns the context
directions before the whitened, high-dimensional class contrast, in the
theorem's terms a large $v_0$ relative to $a_0$ at the encoder rather than
a fast readout. The comparators show what the encoder can do
with the same real spectra when the cause is removed: trained with an
uninformative context it reaches $L^\ast$ only after $15{,}000$--$17{,}000$
steps but with probe gain $+0.35$ (probe $0.92$--$0.93$), reversal
accuracy $0.91$--$0.93$ and context-random accuracy $0.91$--$0.92$; the
frozen encoder gives $0.08$. Excess shifted risk of the joint models over
that comparator is $0.85$. [E3: the $\gamma=10$ variant, which tests
whether the head's rate recovers its effect once the cue no longer
dominates the encoder's gradient.]

\paragraph{Reading.}
With measured spectra the outcome matches the synthetic experiment on
reliance and sharpens the account of suppression: the fitted head uses the
constructed context whenever it is informative, at every width and rate;
whether the encoder learns the spectral contrast before the fit depends on
the balance of gains at the encoder, set jointly by the head's relative
speed (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) and by the contextual cue's amplitude
relative to the spectral one. Both are the theorem's $\kappa Mv_0^2$ seen
from different sides. Excess shifted risk relative to the
uninformative-context comparator is $0.85$ in both regimes.
