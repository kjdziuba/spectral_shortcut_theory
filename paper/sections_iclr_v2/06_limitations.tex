\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
We prove, in a serial linear-encoder model, that a contextual pathway
trained faster than the spectral encoder suppresses the update of the
spectral coefficient at matched fit, that the fitted model fails under contextual reversal
under explicit finite-width conditions, and that readout normalization
removes the width dependence without necessarily removing the failure.
In the synthetic model, slowing the spatial head raises the spectral
cue's linear accessibility at matched loss while the trained classifier
stays strongly context-reliant; a fresh head retrained on decorrelated
context recovers about $0.2$ more shifted accuracy after the slow head
than after the fast one, which yields little more than a random encoder. With measured spectra, high accessibility
coexists with severe reliance, so a probe deficit is not necessary for
failure; with low accessibility the rate response is larger at the
smaller constructed amplitude on tissue and mixed on the public scene. The
paper's claim is that difference, at the stated fits, rates and
constructions, not a general law.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The theorem has a linear encoder, noiseless cues and a fixed spectral skip
coefficient; the experiments add noisy cues and learned routing but keep
a linear encoder in the primary runs (ReLU extension in
Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}) and one head family each. The cues that
show the mechanism are constructed; the natural neighbourhoods tested
showed no accessibility deficit, which does not show natural context
cannot produce one; larger natural receptive fields are untested.
Matched mean training loss is an analogue
of the theorem's matched margin, not the same rule; where a random
encoder already reads the cue the failure is not the theorem's.
Real-spectrum seed variation is conditional on one patient split; the
production-model scope limits are in Appendix~\ref{app:scope}. We test no
mitigation on natural context.
Where the cue was accessible, fresh-head retraining recovered useful
shifted performance despite severe original reliance; with constructed
low accessibility, joint training with uninformative context gave
stronger accessibility and recovery from informative-context encoders
depended on the setting and the procedure; neither a sufficient nor a
necessary repair is identified.
