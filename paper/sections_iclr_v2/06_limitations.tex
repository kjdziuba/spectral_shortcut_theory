\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
%% [E2/E3] Replace the bracketed clause with the supported version once
%% Experiments 2 and 3 report (Astra's sentence, conditional on support):
%% "We prove this mechanism in a linear spectral--spatial model and find
%% corresponding suppression and context-shift failures in the tested
%% nonlinear architectures."
We prove, in a serial linear-encoder model, that a contextual pathway
trained faster than the spectral encoder suppresses the encoder's update
at matched fit, that the fitted model then fails under contextual reversal
while a spectral-only comparator does not, and that readout normalization
removes the width dependence of this effect without removing the failure.
In a ReLU convolutional head over a linear encoder on synthetic data, and
with measured tissue spectra in constructed neighbourhoods, we find the
corresponding suppression at every width and the corresponding failure
under context shift whenever the context is informative, with excess
shifted risk of about $0.75$ (synthetic) and $0.85$ (real spectra) over the
uninformative-context comparator. What transfers with a qualification is
the role of speed: the head's relative rate controls how much of the
spectral cue the encoder learns before the fit, not whether the fitted head
uses the shortcut, and where the contextual cue dominates the encoder's
own gradient even that control disappears.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The theorem has a linear encoder, noiseless cues and a fixed spectral skip
coefficient; the experiments add noisy cues and jointly learned routing, but
keep a linear encoder and one head family each. The contextual cues are
constructed, in the synthetic data by design and in the real-spectrum
experiment explicitly, so neither experiment shows that natural tissue
neighbourhoods produce the effect. Matched mean training loss is an
experimental analogue of the theorem's matched margin, not the same
stopping rule, and the fit threshold is a convention for comparison, not a
training recipe. The initial accessibility asymmetry that the mechanism
needs is set by the bottleneck and the input statistics: where a random
encoder already exposes the spectral cue, as it does for one real class
pair at bottleneck twelve (Section~\ref{sec:real}), the theorem's
$a_0\ge m/2$ case applies and no failure is predicted. Plain gradient
descent with one global rate is the only optimizer studied.

\paragraph{What curvature diagnostics do not tell us.}
On a production spectral--spatial segmentation model we measured the
initialization curvature of the two parameter blocks and found the scalar
ratio inverted ($0.46\pm0.21$ at width $192$) because the inputs are
effectively rank one (effective rank $1.07$ of $942$), and found its growth
with width to be a normalization-gain effect (first-head-convolution slope
$+0.61$ with batch statistics against $+0.006$ with both head BatchNorm
layers at fixed statistics), while the class-contrast direction carries
$12$--$28\times$ less curvature than the dominant input direction
(Appendix~\ref{app:scope}). Scalar curvature therefore does not identify
the mechanism, and directional curvature is not a measurement of impaired
learning; this is why the present paper measures learning outcomes under
interventions rather than curvature.

\paragraph{What a matched retraining study did not show.}
At width $48$ and three seeds, an earlier apparent advantage of freezing
the spectral encoder in that production model came from a comparison that
confounded freezing with gradient-clipping scope and normalization-parameter
treatment. Once those differences were removed, its nominal final-window
advantage was absent, checkpoint selection reversed the ordering within the
original runs, and neither the within-protocol freezing contrasts nor the
change in their gap establishes a population effect (paired $90\%$
intervals in Appendix~\ref{app:scope}). That study cannot identify an
architectural shortcut and does not support a mitigation recommendation;
we make none.

\paragraph{Open problem.}
Whether the mechanism operates in production pipelines trained from scratch
with natural context statistics, where readiness, cue balance and
optimizer all differ from the controlled settings here, is the question
these results leave open.
